# 01 — Carnitine physiology, body economy, and the kidney's OCTN2 conservation system

Dossier for Brief 01. Structured findings: `research/findings_physiology.json` (51 sources, 25 machine-verified claims); all fetched page texts under `research/physiology/pages/`. Footnote format: Title — URL, accessed 2026-11-15.

## 1. Overview

L-carnitine is a small quaternary amine the body treats as a scarce metabolic asset. Its two load-bearing jobs in sustained exertion are (1) shuttling long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for β-oxidation via the carnitine-acylcarnitine cycle — CPT I, CACT, CPT II (Carnitine Inborn Errors of Metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31500110/, accessed 2026-11-15) — and (2) buffering excess acetyl groups as acetylcarnitine via carnitine acetyltransferase, keeping free CoA available when acetyl production outruns TCA-cycle consumption (Carnitine Acetyltransferase Mitigates Metabolic Inertia and Muscle Fatigue during Exercise. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26154055/, accessed 2026-11-15).

The economy is tight: 95% of the pool sits in skeletal muscle (Vegetarians have a reduced skeletal muscle carnitine transport capacity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21753065/, accessed 2026-11-15); skeletal plus cardiac muscle hold ~98% of stores, about 100 mmol in a 70-kg adult (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15); plasma free carnitine is held near 40-50 µmol/L (L-Carnitine — Linus Pauling Institute Micronutrient Information Center — https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine, accessed 2026-11-15). In omnivores ~75% of carnitine is dietary, red meat dominant (Carnitine Inborn Errors of Metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31500110/, accessed 2026-11-15).

The kidney is the conservation organ: free carnitine is filtered at the glomerulus, and more than 90% — 98-99% at baseline — is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule by the sodium-coupled transporter OCTN2 (SLC22A5) (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12908852/, accessed 2026-11-15) (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15). Everything else follows: primary deficiency when OCTN2 is mutated; secondary deficiency when losses overwhelm it (hemodialysis, valproate, pivalates, organic acidemias); and dose-limited supplementation, since gut absorption and tubular reabsorption both saturate (Kinetics, pharmacokinetics, and regulation of L-carnitine and acetyl-L-carnitine metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15591001/, accessed 2026-11-15).

## 2. Evidence in depth

### 2.1 What carnitine does

Long-chain fatty acids cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane as CoA esters; the carnitine-acylcarnitine cycle solves this — CPT I forms acylcarnitine, CACT exchanges it into the matrix, CPT II regenerates acyl-CoA (Carnitine Inborn Errors of Metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31500110/, accessed 2026-11-15). Carnitine acetyltransferase converts acetyl-CoA plus carnitine to acetylcarnitine plus free CoA; disrupting this switch in mice causes acetyl-CoA deficit, perturbed energy charge, and exercise intolerance, reversed by acetylcarnitine (Carnitine Acetyltransferase Mitigates Metabolic Inertia and Muscle Fatigue during Exercise. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26154055/, accessed 2026-11-15). Human biopsy data fit: during incremental exercise muscle acetylcarnitine rises from 6.0 to 15.2 mmol/kg dry weight while free carnitine and CoASH fall (Acetyl group accumulation and pyruvate dehydrogenase activity in human muscle during incremental exercise. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1815472/, accessed 2026-11-15). Carnitine also esterifies accumulating organic acids, protecting the cell from acyl-CoA accretion (Carnitine in Human Muscle Bioenergetics: Can Carnitine Supplementation Improve Physical Exercise? — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31906370/, accessed 2026-11-15).

### 2.2 Body economy

Ninety-five percent of the body carnitine pool resides in skeletal muscle (Vegetarians have a reduced skeletal muscle carnitine transport capacity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21753065/, accessed 2026-11-15); skeletal plus cardiac muscle account for ~98% of stores, estimated at ~100 mmol in a 70-kg adult male (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15) — around 20 g total (Changes in l-carnitine content of fish and meat during domestic cooking. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22062286/, accessed 2026-11-15). Plasma free carnitine runs 40-50 µmol/L, acetyl-L-carnitine 3-6 µmol/L (L-Carnitine — Linus Pauling Institute Micronutrient Information Center — https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine, accessed 2026-11-15). Non-vegetarians obtain ~75% of carnitine from diet (Carnitine Inborn Errors of Metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31500110/, accessed 2026-11-15); beef is the richest source (3-oz steak ~81 mg) (L-Carnitine — Linus Pauling Institute Micronutrient Information Center — https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine, accessed 2026-11-15), salmon holds ~12-fold less, and cooking does not destroy it (Changes in l-carnitine content of fish and meat during domestic cooking. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22062286/, accessed 2026-11-15).

Biosynthesis runs from protein-bound ε-N-trimethyllysine through four enzymes, the last being γ-butyrobetaine dioxygenase (Carnitine Inborn Errors of Metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31500110/, accessed 2026-11-15), which is rate-limiting in liver, kidney, and brain (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15). The human tracer study is species-specific: significant enzyme activity was found in human kidney but not rat kidney, and administered trimethyllysine was converted to carnitine in human kidney (Significance of renal gamma-butyrobetaine hydroxylase for carnitine biosynthesis in man. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6773946/, accessed 2026-11-15) — so the kidney performs the final biosynthetic hydroxylation in humans, alongside liver. Synthesis is ~1.2 µmol/kg/day, 11-34 mg/day at 70 kg (L-Carnitine — Linus Pauling Institute Micronutrient Information Center — https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine, accessed 2026-11-15), with ascorbate as cofactor for both dioxygenases (Ascorbic acid and carnitine biosynthesis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1962562/, accessed 2026-11-15).

### 2.3 Renal handling: the kidney's OCTN2 conservation system (core of this stream)

Free carnitine (161 Da, unbound) is filtered at the glomerulus; more than 90% of the filtered load is reabsorbed by tubular cells, and acylcarnitines are lost in urine far more readily (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15). Baseline renal clearance is 1-3 mL/min against GFR, indicating extensive (98-99%) tubular reabsorption (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12908852/, accessed 2026-11-15); at normal concentrations 90-99% of the filtered load is reclaimed, with saturation kinetics (Kinetics, pharmacokinetics, and regulation of L-carnitine and acetyl-L-carnitine metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15591001/, accessed 2026-11-15). The reabsorption threshold (~40-60 µmol/L) sits right at endogenous plasma levels, so clearance rises toward GFR only when plasma carnitine is supraphysiological (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12908852/, accessed 2026-11-15). Human pharmacokinetics confirm saturability: baseline excretory clearance-to-GFR ratios below 0.1 for endogenous carnitine esters, rising from 0.33 to 5.55 L/h as propionyl-L-carnitine dose increases (Pharmacokinetics of propionyl-L-carnitine in humans: evidence for saturable tubular reabsorption. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11069438/, accessed 2026-11-15); after intravenous dosing 73-95% is excreted unchanged (L-carnitine for valproic acid-induced toxicity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39261302/, accessed 2026-11-15).

OCTN2 is a 557-amino-acid Na+-coupled, high-affinity carnitine transporter (Km ~4.34 µM), strongly expressed in kidney, skeletal muscle, heart, and placenta (Molecular and functional identification of sodium ion-dependent, high affinity human carnitine transporter OCTN2. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9685390/, accessed 2026-11-15). In kidney it co-localizes with the PDZK1 scaffold in brush-border membranes, which substantially enhances transport activity (SLC22A5 (OCTN2) Carnitine Transporter-Indispensable for Cell Metabolism, a Jekyll and Hyde of Human Cancer. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6982704/, accessed 2026-11-15).

Genetics proves the system's importance: SLC22A5 mutations cause primary systemic carnitine deficiency — hypoketotic hypoglycemia or cardiomyopathy in children, sudden death in children and adults (Functional and molecular studies in primary carnitine deficiency. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28841266/, accessed 2026-11-15); the original study found four mutations in three pedigrees, and the jvs mouse missense abrogates transport (Primary systemic carnitine deficiency is caused by mutations in a gene encoding sodium ion-dependent carnitine transporter. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9916797/, accessed 2026-11-15). The phenotype is renal: intracellular accumulation falls, urinary losses rise, serum falls (Carnitine transport and fatty acid oxidation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26828774/, accessed 2026-11-15). Across 621 published cases, cardiac symptoms (predominantly cardiomyopathy) were most prevalent, 23.8% (Clinical characteristics of primary carnitine deficiency: A structured review using a case-by-case approach. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34997761/, accessed 2026-11-15).

Muscle uptake is insulin-dependent: hyperinsulinemia raised muscle carnitine from 22.0 to 24.7 mmol/kg dry weight with a 2.3-fold OCTN2 mRNA rise, while hypercarnitinemia alone had no effect (Insulin stimulates L-carnitine accumulation in human skeletal muscle. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16368715/, accessed 2026-11-15). Co-ingested whey protein blunts the response (Protein ingestion acutely inhibits insulin-stimulated muscle carnitine uptake in healthy young men. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26675771/, accessed 2026-11-15); caffeine, a Na+/K+ ATPase stimulus, accelerates plasma carnitine clearance (Caffeine ingestion stimulates plasma carnitine clearance in humans. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36806708/, accessed 2026-11-15). Vegetarians — 16% lower plasma carnitine, 17% lower muscle carnitine, reduced OCTN2, yet greater whole-body retention of an oral dose — show that the adaptive reserve sits in the kidney (Vegetarians have a reduced skeletal muscle carnitine transport capacity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21753065/, accessed 2026-11-15).

### 2.4 What regulates OCTN2

Documented regulators are pre-clinical: PPARα activation (fasting, Wy-14643) enhances carnitine biosynthesis and hepatic OCTN2-mediated import in mice (PPAR alpha-activation results in enhanced carnitine biosynthesis and OCTN2-mediated hepatic carnitine accumulation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17692817/, accessed 2026-11-15); PGC-1α and MEF2 drive SLC22A5 in muscle cells (PGC-1α and MEF2 Regulate the Transcription of the Carnitine Transporter OCTN2 Gene in C2C12 Cells and in Mouse Skeletal Muscle. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36293168/, accessed 2026-11-15); IFN-γ and TNF-α increase intestinal OCTN2 in mice (Cytokine regulation of OCTN2 expression and activity in small and large intestine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20722056/, accessed 2026-11-15); mechanical stimulation drives OCTN2 through the YAP/Tead4 pathway in sarcopenia models (Mechanical Stimulation Induces Yap Mediated OCTN2 Transcription to Enhance Carnitine Metabolism in Sarcopenia. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40958655/, accessed 2026-11-15). For sepsis, no human renal OCTN2 regulation data exist — SLC22A5 is flagged in septic-AKI macrophage work (Targeting SLC22A5 fosters mitophagy inhibition-mediated macrophage immunity against septic acute kidney injury upon CD47-SIRPα axis blockade. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38586373/, accessed 2026-11-15).

### 2.5 Forms and pharmacology

Forms: L-carnitine base; L-tartrate, the salt used in trials (4.5 g tartrate delivers 3 g base) (Protein ingestion acutely inhibits insulin-stimulated muscle carnitine uptake in healthy young men. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26675771/, accessed 2026-11-15); acetyl-L-carnitine, with oral pharmacokinetics characterized in renal impairment (Pharmacokinetics of oral acetyl-L-carnitine in renal impairment. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2340852/, accessed 2026-11-15); propionyl-L-carnitine (Pharmacokinetics of propionyl-L-carnitine in humans: evidence for saturable tubular reabsorption. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11069438/, accessed 2026-11-15); and glycine propionyl-L-carnitine, which acutely raises plasma nitrate/nitrite in resistance-trained men (Glycine propionyl-L-carnitine increases plasma nitrate/nitrite in resistance trained men. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18053183/, accessed 2026-11-15). Oral bioavailability is dose-dependent: dietary carnitine 54-87%, 0.5-6 g supplements only 14-18% (Kinetics, pharmacokinetics, and regulation of L-carnitine and acetyl-L-carnitine metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15591001/, accessed 2026-11-15); reviews give 5-18% for 1-6 g versus up to ~75% dietary (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12908852/, accessed 2026-11-15). Unabsorbed supplement is degraded by large-intestine microorganisms (Kinetics, pharmacokinetics, and regulation of L-carnitine and acetyl-L-carnitine metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15591001/, accessed 2026-11-15). Whole-body turnover at normal intake is 38-119 h (Kinetics, pharmacokinetics, and regulation of L-carnitine and acetyl-L-carnitine metabolism. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15591001/, accessed 2026-11-15); exogenous propionyl-L-carnitine has a plasma half-life of ~1.09 h (Pharmacokinetics of propionyl-L-carnitine in humans: evidence for saturable tubular reabsorption. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11069438/, accessed 2026-11-15).

### 2.6 Deficiency states

Primary deficiency: OCTN2 failure as above (Functional and molecular studies in primary carnitine deficiency. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28841266/, accessed 2026-11-15). Hemodialysis removes ~70-100 mg into the dialysate per session (Carnitine Deficiency in Chronic Kidney Disease: Pathophysiology, Clinical Implications, and Therapeutic Perspectives. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251438/, accessed 2026-11-15); plasma free L-carnitine falls from 19.5 to 5.6 µmol/L across a session (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine in patients with end-stage renal disease undergoing long-term hemodialysis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11014405/, accessed 2026-11-15); in 150 dialysis patients, deficiency (free <20 µmol/L) was 25.3% and insufficiency (acyl/free >0.4) 86.7% (Prevalence of Carnitine Deficiency and Decreased Carnitine Levels in Patients on Hemodialysis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30943487/, accessed 2026-11-15). Valproic acid chronically produces hyperammonemia and hepatotoxicity, with evidence supporting L-carnitine treatment (L-carnitine for valproic acid-induced toxicity. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39261302/, accessed 2026-11-15); pivalate-conjugated antibiotics depleted carnitine in valproate-treated epilepsy patients, prevented by supplementation (Carnitine supplementation prevents carnitine deficiency caused by pivalate-conjugated antibiotics in patients with epilepsy prescribed valproate. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33714931/, accessed 2026-11-15). In organic acidurias, accumulating acids are trapped as coenzyme A, carnitine, and glycine esters, and carnitine supplementation is standard maintenance (Organic acidurias in adults: late complications and management. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29335813/, accessed 2026-11-15). Vegetarian adults and children have significantly lower plasma carnitine (Carnitine status of lactoovovegetarians and strict vegetarian adults and children. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2756917/, accessed 2026-11-15), but synthesis plus efficient reabsorption prevent deficiency in healthy strict vegetarians (L-Carnitine — Linus Pauling Institute Micronutrient Information Center — https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/L-carnitine, accessed 2026-11-15).

## 3. Human evidence

Load-bearing numbers rest on direct human data: exercise-biopsy acetylcarnitine kinetics (Acetyl group accumulation and pyruvate dehydrogenase activity in human muscle during incremental exercise. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1815472/, accessed 2026-11-15); insulin-clamp muscle carnitine and OCTN2 measurements (Insulin stimulates L-carnitine accumulation in human skeletal muscle. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16368715/, accessed 2026-11-15); clearance pharmacokinetics in hemodialysis patients (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine in patients with end-stage renal disease undergoing long-term hemodialysis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11014405/, accessed 2026-11-15); dialysis-cohort prevalence (Prevalence of Carnitine Deficiency and Decreased Carnitine Levels in Patients on Hemodialysis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30943487/, accessed 2026-11-15); case-level OCTN2 genetics (Functional and molecular studies in primary carnitine deficiency. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28841266/, accessed 2026-11-15); and a pivalate-antibiotic supplementation study (Carnitine supplementation prevents carnitine deficiency caused by pivalate-conjugated antibiotics in patients with epilepsy prescribed valproate. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33714931/, accessed 2026-11-15). The 98-99% reabsorption figure derives from a review of human clearance data (Pharmacokinetics of L-carnitine. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12908852/, accessed 2026-11-15). Sepsis supplementation evidence is human but negative for mortality (The supplementation of L-carnitine in critically ill patients with sepsis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39367436/, accessed 2026-11-15).

## 6. Open questions for the parent

- Multi-day desert exertion with dehydration: effects on filtered carnitine load and OCTN2 reabsorption are untested — brief 03 (renal stress) owns this junction.
- OCTN2 missense variants (150 now cataloged) imply interindividual variation in renal conservation; should the review flag low-conservation phenotypes for screening?
- Any trail supplementation protocol must respect the insulin-dependence of muscle uptake (carbohydrate co-ingestion) and the dose-dependent bioavailability ceiling.
- Unabsorbed oral carnitine feeds gut microbes (brief 05's TMAO arm); whether sustained exertion shifts the urinary acyl/free balance is untested.
