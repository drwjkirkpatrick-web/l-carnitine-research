Acute and Chronic L-Carnitine Supplementation Enhances Endurance Performance 
Without Consistent Effects on Substrate Oxidation in Active Individuals: A 
Systematic Review.

Eryiğit H(1), Güzel Y(1), Bulut S(1), Turnagöl HH(1), Atakan MM(1).

Author information:
(1)Division of Exercise Nutrition and Metabolism, Faculty of Sport Sciences, 
Hacettepe University, Ankara, Türkiye.

OBJECTIVES: This study aims to systematically evaluate the effects of acute and 
chronic L-carnitine supplementation on endurance performance and substrate 
oxidation during exercise among active individuals.
METHODS: This systematic review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for 
Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. A total of 647 studies 
were initially retrieved from PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, 
Scopus, Ovid MEDLINE, EBSCO Open Dissertations, EBSCO Academic Search Ultimate, 
and SPORTDiscus databases, with searches conducted from database inception to 
February 1, 2026. Randomized placebo-controlled crossover and parallel-group 
trials were eligible for inclusion. Studies meeting the inclusion criteria were 
assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool, with the 
crossover-specific version applied to randomized crossover trials.
RESULTS: A total of 18 studies met the inclusion criteria. Overall, 15 (83%) 
studies were rated as having some concerns, three (17%) were classified as 
having a high risk of bias, and none were judged to be at low risk of bias. Ten 
studies investigated acute L-carnitine supplementation (2-15 g), seven studies 
examined chronic supplementation (2-3 g/day for 2-24 weeks), and one study 
evaluated both acute and chronic supplementation (3 g on a single day and over 
2 weeks). Both acute and chronic L-carnitine supplementation were associated 
with improvements in endurance performance following acute supplementation and 
chronic supplementation. By contrast, no significant changes in fat oxidation 
during exercise were reported after either acute (2-4 g ingested 120-180 min 
before exercise) or chronic supplementation (2 g/day for 2-4 weeks).
CONCLUSIONS: L-carnitine may enhance selected endurance performance outcomes 
among active individuals; however, its reputation as a "fat-burning" ergogenic 
aid is not supported by current evidence, following short-term supplementation.
